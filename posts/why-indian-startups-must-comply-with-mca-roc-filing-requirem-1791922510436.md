# Why Indian Startups Must Comply with MCA/ROC Filing Requirements Pre-Revenue

**Quick answer:**  
- **Legal mandate**: Indian startups must file with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) and Registrar of Companies (ROC) under the Companies Act, 2013, regardless of revenue status.  
- **Statutory obligation**: Non-compliance risks penalties, deregistration, and loss of legal entity status, even for pre-revenue businesses.  

## Why is MCA/ROC filing mandatory for Indian startups pre-revenue?  
- **Answer**: Mandatory due to statutory incorporation requirements under the Companies Act, 2013.  
- Upon incorporation, a startup becomes a legal entity under Section 3 of the Companies Act, triggering mandatory filings like Form INC-22 (registered office address), DIR-12 (director appointments), and annual returns (Form MGT-7/AOC-4). The MCA mandates these filings to maintain active status, irrespective of revenue. As of 2024, penalties for non-compliance start at ₹200 per day under Section 12, with potential prosecution for recurring defaults.  

## What are the consequences of non-compliance for Indian startups?  
- **Answer**: Non-compliance results in financial penalties, loss of legal existence, and operational disruptions.  
- The ROC imposes escalating fines for late filings: ₹200 per day for Forms MGT-7 and AOC-4 (MCA Circular 17/2021). Severe defaults can lead to strike-off under Section 248, deregistering the company. In 2023, over 22,000 companies were struck off for non-compliance (MCA Annual Report 2023). Deregistration invalidates contracts, blocks banking access, and disqualifies founders from future directorships.
