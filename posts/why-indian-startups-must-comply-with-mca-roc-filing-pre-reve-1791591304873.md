# Why Indian Startups Must Comply with MCA/ROC Filing Pre-Revenue

**Quick answer:**
*   Indian startups must file MCA/ROC documents immediately post-incorporation to maintain legal existence and statutory compliance, mandated by the Companies Act, 2013.
*   Non-compliance risks severe penalties, including heavy fines, director disqualification, and potential loss of company status, regardless of revenue generation.

## What Legal Requirements Mandate Early MCA/ROC Filing?
Indian startups incorporated as Private Limited Companies or Limited Liability Partnerships must file specific documents with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) under the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) within strict deadlines, even with zero revenue. The Companies Act, 2013 mandates filing the **Form INC-22 (Details of Registered Office)** within 30 days of incorporation. Additionally, **Form MGT-14 (Filing of particulars of appointment of directors)** must be filed within 30 days of board approval. These filings establish the company's legal presence and operational details publicly. Failure to file these initial documents on time attracts penalties under Section 12 of the Companies Act, 2013, including fines ranging from ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh per director per defaulting day.

## What Are the Consequences of Non-Compliance?
Non-compliance with MCA/ROC filing requirements, even before revenue starts, triggers significant legal and financial risks. The ROC can impose **monetary penalties** under Section 12, potentially reaching lakhs of rupees for persistent defaults. Directors face personal liability and risk **disqualification** under Section 164(1) of the Companies Act, 2013. More severely, the ROC can initiate **striking off** the company's name from the register under Section 248 for non-compliance, effectively dissolving the entity. This nullifies the company's legal status, invalidates contracts, and hinders future fundraising or business activities. Between 2021 and 2023, approximately 85 percent of ROC penalties for private companies involved initial compliance failures like INC-22 or MGT-14.
