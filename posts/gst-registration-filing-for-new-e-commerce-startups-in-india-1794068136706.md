# GST Registration & Filing for New E-commerce Startups in India

**Quick answer:**
*   Mandatory GST registration applies to e-commerce operators supplying goods/services *within* India, starting from the first Rs. 20 lakh aggregate turnover threshold (Rs. 10 lakh for special category states).
*   Registration requires PAN, Aadhaar, bank details, business proof, and e-commerce marketplace details; typically completed within 3-7 working days post-document submission.
*   Compliance involves monthly/quarterly returns (GSTR-3B, GSTR-1), tax collection at source (TCS) on marketplace sales, and annual returns (GSTR-9).

## Who needs GST registration for e-commerce?
E-commerce operators supplying goods or services *within India* must register for GST if their aggregate turnover crosses Rs. 20 lakh in a financial year (Rs. 10 lakh for special category states like Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, etc.). Registration is mandatory *from the first day* of business if the expected turnover exceeds this threshold. Even startups below the threshold can opt for voluntary registration to avail Input Tax Credit (ITC). Non-resident e-commerce operators supplying services to Indian consumers also require mandatory registration, regardless of turnover. Registration ties the e-commerce platform and its sellers to the GST framework, ensuring tax collection on digital sales.

## How is GST registration for e-commerce completed?
GST registration for an e-commerce startup involves submitting an application (Form GST REG-01) on the GST portal with PAN, Aadhaar, bank account details, business address proof, and details of e-commerce marketplaces if selling via one. The process typically takes 3-7 working days post-document verification. Startups must provide a digital signature for the application. Once registered, a unique GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) is issued, along with a registration certificate. Platforms like filingpro.io streamline this process for startups, handling document preparation, portal filing, and follow-up verification specifically for e-commerce businesses, ensuring compliance with GST registration norms.

## What are the GST filing compliance requirements for e-commerce?
E-commerce startups must file monthly returns (GSTR-3B) by the 20th of the following month and outward supply returns (GSTR-1) by the 11th of the following month. They must collect Tax Collected at Source (TCS) @ 1% on sales made via marketplaces and deposit it with the government. Annual returns (GSTR-9) are due by the 31st of December of the next financial year. Maintaining accurate digital records of all transactions and ITC claims is critical.
