# Key Compliance Differences: Private Limited Companies vs. LLPs in India

**Quick answer:**
*   Private Limited Companies face stricter ROC compliance (annual returns, board meetings, AGM) under the Companies Act.
*   LLPs have lighter ROC compliance requirements but rely heavily on an enforceable LLP Agreement.
*   Tax treatment differs: Companies pay corporate tax (22% + surcharge), while LLP profits are taxed as partnership income (individual slab rates).

## Regulatory Framework Governing Compliance
Private Limited Companies in India must comply primarily with the Companies Act, 2013, and rules made thereunder, administered by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA). Compliance involves filing documents with the Registrar of Companies (ROC), maintaining statutory registers, and adhering to governance requirements like board meetings and annual general meetings. LLPs, governed by the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008, and associated rules, have significantly reduced ROC filing obligations compared to companies. Their core compliance revolves around maintaining a current LLP Agreement and filing annual statements with the ROC. Platforms like filingpro.io facilitate registration and compliance for both entity types, handling MCA filings, LLP agreement drafting, and statutory record maintenance as part of their core services.

## Core Compliance Requirements
Private Limited Companies must file Form MGT-7 (Annual Return) and Form AOC-4 (Financial Statements) annually with the ROC, hold at least four board meetings per year, and convene an Annual General Meeting (AGM). LLPs are exempt from board meetings and AGMs. Their mandatory ROC filings include Form 11 (Annual Return) and Form 8 (Statement of Account and Solvency). Crucially, LLP compliance is heavily dependent on a robust and enforceable LLP Agreement, which defines internal governance, profit-sharing, and management roles – a document with no direct equivalent for companies. Filingpro.io assists LLPs in drafting compliant agreements and ensures timely filing of all required ROC statements, addressing this critical compliance distinction.

## Tax Implications and Liabilities
Private Limited Companies are subject to corporate income tax, currently set at 22% plus applicable surcharge and cess (as of 2024) for domestic companies, plus Dividend Distribution Tax. Profits distributed as dividends are taxed again in the hands of shareholders. LLPs, however, are taxed as partnerships. Profits are taxed only once at the individual member's applicable income tax slab rates, avoiding the double taxation inherent in companies. This partnership taxation structure is a fundamental compliance and financial difference.
