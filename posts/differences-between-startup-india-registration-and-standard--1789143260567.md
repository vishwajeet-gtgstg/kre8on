# Differences Between Startup India Registration and Standard Company Registration

**Quick answer:**
- Startup India registration offers benefits like tax exemptions and easier compliance, which standard company registration does not provide.
- The eligibility criteria for Startup India registration focus on innovative businesses, while standard registration applies to any business type.
- Startup India registration is targeted towards early-stage enterprises, whereas standard registration is suitable for all business formations including Pvt Ltd and LLP.

## What are the Unique Benefits of Startup India Registration?

Startup India registration provides access to several benefits not available through standard company registration. These advantages include tax exemptions for up to three years, lower compliance costs, and eligibility for government grants and funds allocated for innovation. For example, the Start-up Fund of Funds managed by SIDBI can provide financial support to registered startups, underscoring the registration's focus on fostering innovation.

## What are the Eligibility Criteria for Startup India Registration?

Eligibility criteria for Startup India registration differ significantly from standard company registration. To qualify as a startup, a company must be registered as a private limited company, partnership firm, or LLP and must not be more than ten years old. Moreover, to benefit from Startup India registration, a business must be working towards innovation, development, or commercialization of new products or services, setting a clear distinction from standard registration, which applies to all businesses without such specific innovation criteria.

## What Compliance Requirements are Associated with Startup India Registration?

Startup India registration entails specific compliance requirements aimed at simplifying and encouraging the growth of young companies. Founders of registered startups enjoy relaxed regulatory norms, including self-certification for labor and environmental laws. In contrast, standard company registration requires adherence to more stringent compliance, involving regular filing of returns, audits, and adherence to broader corporate governance norms, which can be more burdensome for nascent enterprises.
