# How to Register a Sole Proprietorship in India

Registering a sole proprietorship in India involves specific steps focused on establishing the business identity and obtaining necessary compliance registrations. The core process centers on selecting a unique business name and registering it formally, followed by securing mandatory tax and operational registrations.

## What is the Sole Proprietorship Registration Process?
The **sole proprietorship registration** process primarily involves formalizing the business name and securing basic operational registrations. First, choose a unique business name that isn't already registered or trademarked. Next, register this name under relevant local regulations: this often includes obtaining a Shop & Establishment Act license from the state municipal corporation where the business operates, which is mandatory for most physical businesses. Additionally, register the proprietorship under the Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSME) scheme via the Udyam portal to access benefits and establish credibility. While not a central government registration like a Private Limited Company, these steps provide legal recognition for the business entity.

## What are the Costs for Sole Proprietorship Registration?
The costs associated with **registering a sole proprietorship** are relatively low compared to other business structures. The primary expenses include government fees for the Shop & Establishment Act license (varying by state, typically ₹500 to ₹5,000) and MSME registration (free on the Udyam portal). ## What are the Key Compliance Requirements for Sole Proprietorships?
Post-registration, **sole proprietorship** owners must adhere to several key compliance requirements. Mandatory GST registration is required if the annual turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states). Professional Tax registration is mandatory in most states for employers and self-employed individuals. An Aadhaar-linked current bank account must be opened in the exact business name. Income tax returns (ITR) must be filed annually using ITR-4 (Sugam) if turnover exceeds ₹60 lakh.
