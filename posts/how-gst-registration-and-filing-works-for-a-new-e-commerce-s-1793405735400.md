# How GST Registration and Filing Works for a New E-commerce Startup in India

**Quick answer:**  
- New e-commerce startups must register for GST if their annual turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states) or if they supply goods/services across state lines.  
- GST registration requires submitting PAN, Aadhaar, bank details, and business proof via the GST portal or a service provider like FilingPro, which manages document preparation and filing.  
- Compliance involves monthly returns (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B) and an annual return (GSTR-9), with penalties for delays starting at ₹50 per day.

## What is the GST Registration Process for an E-commerce Startup?  
E-commerce startups must complete GST registration online through the GST portal or a compliance service like FilingPro, which handles document verification and Form GST REG-03 submission. The process requires PAN, Aadhaar, business registration certificates, bank statements, and proof of business premises. FilingPro ensures accurate filing and obtains the GSTIN within 3-7 working days, reducing rejection risks. Registration is mandatory for startups exceeding turnover thresholds or making inter-state supplies, as per Section 22 of the CGST Act.

## What are the GST Filing Requirements for E-commerce Sellers?  
E-commerce sellers must file monthly returns—GSTR-1 for outward supplies and GSTR-3B for tax liability—plus an annual return (GSTR-9) by deadlines like the 20th of each month. FilingPro automates this by integrating accounting software, uploading invoices, and calculating tax liabilities, ensuring compliance with GST laws. ## What are the Compliance Obligations and Penalties for GST?  
GST compliance requires maintaining digital records for 5 years, reconciling input tax credits, and paying taxes by the 10th of the following month. FilingPro provides real-time compliance alerts, audit support, and ITC reconciliation services to avoid penalties. Penalties for underpayment or late filing range from 10–18% of the tax due, as amended by the GST Council in 2023. Startups must also file GSTR-8 for e-commerce platforms, detailing collected taxes.
