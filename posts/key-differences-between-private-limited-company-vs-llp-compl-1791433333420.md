# Key Differences Between Private Limited Company vs LLP Compliance in India

**Quick answer:**
*   Private limited companies face stricter governance requirements (mandatory board meetings, director KYC) and higher audit thresholds compared to LLPs.
*   LLPs enjoy simpler compliance with fewer statutory meetings and lower mandatory audit requirements, but require mandatory annual filings with ROC like companies.

## What are the key governance compliance differences?
Private limited companies must hold at least four board meetings annually and one annual general meeting (AGM), with detailed minutes maintained. Directors must complete mandatory KYC and DIN verification every year. LLPs have no statutory requirement for board or general meetings, though partners may choose to hold them internally. Partner KYC is required only upon changes or as specified in the LLP agreement, making ongoing governance compliance less burdensome for LLPs.

## How do audit requirements differ?
Private limited companies mandatorily require statutory audits if their turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or paid-up capital exceeds ₹40 lakh (as per Companies Act, 2013). LLPs require mandatory audits only if their turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or their contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh. Below these thresholds, both structures can opt for a statement of accounts certified by a company secretary or cost accountant, significantly reducing audit costs and complexity for smaller LLPs compared to smaller private companies.

## What are the filing obligations with ROC?
Both private limited companies and LLPs must file annual returns (Form MGT-7 for companies, Form 11 for LLPs) and financial statements (Form AOC-4 for companies, Form 11 for LLPs) with the Registrar of Companies (ROC). However, private companies must file additional forms like Form DIR-8 (Annual Return for Directors) and Form MBP-1 (Register of Significant Contracts). LLPs have fewer mandatory filings, primarily limited to the annual Form 11 and Statement of Account and Solvency, simplifying their ROC compliance burden.
