# GST Registration and Filing for New E-commerce Startups in India

**Quick answer:**
*   New e-commerce startups must register for GST if their turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states) or if they supply goods/services through an e-commerce operator.
*   GST filing is mandatory monthly (GSTR-1 for outward supplies, GSTR-3B for summary) and annually (GSTR-9).
*   Compliance requires maintaining accurate records, issuing proper invoices, and adhering to reverse charge mechanisms for marketplace sales.

## What are the GST registration thresholds for new e-commerce startups?
New e-commerce startups in India must register for GST if their aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh in a financial year (₹10 lakh for special category states like the North-Eastern states, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Jammu & Kashmir, and Ladakh). Crucially, even if the startup's own turnover is below this threshold, **mandatory registration applies** if the startup supplies goods or services through an e-commerce operator (like Amazon, Flipkart, or a marketplace) and the operator is required to collect tax at source (TCS) under Section 52 of the CGST Act. Registration must be applied for online via the GST portal once the liability arises.

## How often does an e-commerce startup file GST returns?
An e-commerce startup files GST returns monthly and annually. The core monthly filings are **GSTR-3B**, a summary return due by the 20th of the following month, reporting total outward supplies, input tax credit claimed, and tax payable. Additionally, **GSTR-1** detailing outward supplies (including those made via marketplaces) must be filed monthly by the 11th of the following month. For sales made through an e-commerce operator (marketplace), the operator files a separate **GSTR-8** reporting the TCS collected. An annual return, **GSTR-9**, consolidating the entire year's data, is due by the 31st of December of the next financial year. Missing deadlines attracts penalties.

## What key compliance essentials must an e-commerce startup follow?
E-commerce startups must maintain detailed records of all transactions, including invoices, bills of supply, and e-commerce operator statements. They must issue proper GST-compliant invoices for B2B sales and tax invoices for B2C sales. For sales made through an e-commerce marketplace, the startup must provide the operator with their GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) and details of supplies. The startup must also pay any tax liability, including tax payable under the reverse charge mechanism (RCM) applicable to certain services or goods purchased from unregistered suppliers.
