# GST Registration and Filing for New E-commerce Startups in India: A Complete Guide

**Quick answer:**  
- New e-commerce startups with turnover exceeding ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states) must register for GST within 30 days of business commencement.  
- Mandatory quarterly GST filings (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B) require digital signatures and PAN-linked bank accounts, with penalties for delays.  
- E-commerce platforms must deduct TCS at 1% on supplies and file Form GSTR-8 if aggregate sales exceed ₹20 lakh annually.  

## Who Must Register for GST?  
E-commerce startups must register for GST if their annual turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special category states like Himachal Pradesh) or if they supply goods/services across state borders. Startups selling through platforms like Amazon or Flipkart must register even if turnover is below ₹20 lakh, as per Section 24 of the CGST Act. Registration is mandatory within 30 days of business commencement. Failure to register attracts a penalty of ₹10,000 plus 10% of tax evaded.  

## How to Complete GST Registration?  
Registration requires submitting Form GST REG-10 online via the GST portal with documents like PAN, Aadhaar, bank details, and business address proof. Digital signatures are mandatory for directors/partners. Approval typically takes 3-7 working days post-document verification. ## How to File GST Returns?  
E-commerce startups must file GSTR-3B (summary returns) quarterly by the 20th of the next month, and GSTR-1 (outward supplies) monthly by the 11th. Additionally, platforms must file Form GSTR-8 (TCS details) quarterly. Late incurs a ₹200/day penalty per return.
