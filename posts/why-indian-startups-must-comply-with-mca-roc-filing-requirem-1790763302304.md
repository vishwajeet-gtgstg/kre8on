# Why Indian Startups Must Comply with MCA/ROC Filing Requirements Before Revenue

**Quick answer:**
*   MCA/ROC compliance starts upon company registration, not revenue generation.
*   Mandatory filings (e.g., Form INC-20A, annual returns) are legal obligations under the Companies Act, 2013.
*   Non-compliance risks penalties, director disqualification, and loss of legal protections.

## What triggers MCA/ROC filing obligations for startups?
Indian startups trigger MCA/ROC filing obligations immediately upon incorporation as a Private Limited Company, LLP, or OPC under the Companies Act, 2013. Registration itself creates the legal entity, imposing statutory duties like filing Form INC-20A (declaration of commencement of business) within 180 days, regardless of revenue activity. These obligations exist independently of financial performance.

## What key filings are required pre-revenue?
Pre-revenue, mandatory MCA/ROC filings include Form INC-20A (commencement declaration), Form DIR-2 (consent of directors), Form INC-22 (registered office address), and maintaining statutory registers. Annual filings like Form MGT-7 (annual return) and Form AOC-4 (financial statements) become due within 30 days of AGM, even with nil operations. These filings establish the company's legal standing and transparency.

## What are the consequences of non-compliance?
Non-compliance with MCA/ROC requirements pre-revenue attracts penalties under Section 12 of the Companies Act, 2013. Penalties can range from ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh per default, with potential fines for directors. Severe cases risk director disqualification under Section 164(1), loss of "Startup India" benefits, and even company dissolution.
