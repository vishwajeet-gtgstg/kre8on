# Why Indian Startups Must Comply with MCA/ROC Filings Pre-Revenue

**Quick answer:**
*   MCA/ROC compliance is mandatory for *all* registered Indian companies, regardless of revenue, per the Companies Act, 2013.
*   Failure to file statutory documents like Form MGT-7 or Form AOC-4 results in penalties, director disqualification, and potential company strike-off.

## Why is MCA/ROC Compliance Mandatory Before Revenue?
Indian startups incorporated as Private Limited Companies or Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs) are legal entities distinct from their founders. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA), via the Registrar of Companies (ROC), mandates specific filings annually, irrespective of financial activity. These filings include financial statements (Form AOC-4), annual returns (Form MGT-7), and other declarations. Non-compliance attracts severe penalties, including fines up to ₹10 lakh per violation, director disqualification for up to 7 years under Section 102 of the Companies Act, 2013, and the ultimate risk of the company being struck off the register by the ROC, invalidating its legal existence.

## What Key MCA/ROC Filings Apply Pre-Revenue?
Even with zero revenue, registered companies must file core documents annually. These include Form AOC-4 (Financial Statements), Form MGT-7 (Annual Return), Form INC-22A (Active Company Compliance), and Form DIR-8 (Declaration by Independent Directors). For startups, Form INC-22A confirming the registered office address is particularly crucial. Missing deadlines, even without transactions, triggers automatic penalties and penalties escalate with each default. ## What Happens if Startups Ignore Pre-Revenue Filing Requirements?
Ignoring MCA/ROC filing obligations pre-revenue triggers immediate consequences. The ROC imposes late fees and penalties starting from ₹200 per day for delayed Form AOC-4 filing. Directors face personal liability, including potential disqualification under Section 102, preventing them from holding any directorship in other companies. The company risks being marked "Inactive" or "Struck Off" by the ROC, leading to its legal dissolution and inability to operate, open bank accounts, or raise funds.
