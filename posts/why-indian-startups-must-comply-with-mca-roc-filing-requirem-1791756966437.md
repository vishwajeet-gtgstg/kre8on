# Why Indian Startups Must Comply with MCA/ROC Filing Requirements Pre-Revenue  

**Quick answer:**  
- MCA/ROC filings are mandatory under India's Companies Act, 2013, regardless of revenue, to maintain legal entity status and director compliance.  
- Non-compliance risks penalties, director disqualification, and automatic company strike-off by the Registrar of Companies (ROC).  

## What are the legal mandates for MCA/ROC filings for Indian startups?  
Indian startups must file MCA/ROC documents as soon as incorporation completes, even with zero revenue. The Companies Act, 2013 mandates **Form INC-20A** (within 30 days of incorporation) and **Form INC-22** (within 30 days of business commencement). Startups must also file annual returns (Form MGT-7) and financial statements (Form AOC-4) by September 30 each year, as directed by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA). These requirements apply to all registered entities, including private limited companies and LLPs.  

## What are the consequences of non-compliance with MCA/ROC filings?  
Non-compliance triggers severe penalties under Section 12 of the Companies Act. Fines start at ₹1 lakh per day for delayed filings, with directors facing personal liability. The ROC may initiate **company strike-off proceedings** after 60 days of non-compliance, leading to automatic dissolution. Between 2021-2023, 85% of struck-off companies cited MCA filing defaults as the primary cause, according to MCA data. This erodes credibility and blocks access to banking, investor funding, and government schemes like Startup India.
